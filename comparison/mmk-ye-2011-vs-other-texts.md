# Ye Shaoyong's critical text of the Mūlamadhyamakakārikā compared with the other Sanskrit texts

Verse-by-verse comparison of the Sanskrit of Ye Shaoyong (2011) (445 verses, the text in this repository) with three other Sanskrit texts of the work on MEOBTIS:

- **GRETIL**: keyed by Douglas Bachman, 448 verses numbered `MMK_c.v`; its header does not name the edition it follows. The BDRC e-text `P00AD3515` has exactly the same text (compared with whitespace removed), so it is not counted separately.
- **DSBC**: the romanised text of J. W. de Jong's edition (Adyar), 445 verses, input by Miroj Shakya in 2023.
- **BDRC critical text** (`P743000B8`, a text in six layers, one per group of chapters; its metadata names no editor), 448 verses.

**Method.** Each text was split into verses by chapter. Verses were aligned in order by similarity, so that a verse that is numbered differently or moved is still matched (the numbering differs from chapter 1 onward). The comparison ignores word division, punctuation, Ye's circumflex sandhi marks (`â`, `î`, `ô`) and `ṁ` against `ṃ`. A verse is "identical" if the normalised text is the same; "orthographic" if it differs only in vowel length, anusvāra or nasal and the avagraha; otherwise it is listed as a "variant". The lists below are mechanical: they show where the readings differ, not which reading is better. In the lists, `[A→B]` means that Ye has `A` where the other text has `B`, with a few preceding letters for orientation (word divisions removed).

| | GRETIL | BDRC critical text | DSBC |
|---|---|---|---|
| Identical to Ye (after normalising) | 342 | 336 | 314 |
| Orthographic differences only | 35 | 35 | 38 |
| Variant readings | 66 | 72 | 91 |
| Verse numbered or divided differently | 7 | 7 | 4 |

Two things stand out. First, GRETIL and the BDRC critical text agree with each other almost everywhere (they differ from Ye in the same verses and in the same way), and the verse counts of the two are the same (448), so they probably go back to the same edition; neither names it. The DSBC text, which names J. W. de Jong's edition, has the same text in 445 verses. Second, the DSBC text differs from Ye in many more verses than the other two (38 verses where only the DSBC text differs), so most of its extra variants are particular to its keying or to the manuscript or edition it follows.

**Verse order and division.** Ye's chapter 1 has the verses 1.2 and 1.3 of the other texts in the opposite order (his notes say that the commentaries from Buddhapālita onward exchange them, and his text follows that order). In chapter 21 the verse division differs (21.8–21.11). The texts based on the older numbering have one more verse than Ye's numbered text in each of chapters 3 (3.7, in GRETIL and the BDRC text only), 12 (12.6) and 23 (23.20), so their later verses are numbered one higher. Ye prints the chapter 12 verse as a starred verse, "12.5*" (`parapudgalajaṃ duḥkhaṃ yadi kaḥ parapudgalaḥ …`), noting that it is in the Sanskrit manuscript, de Jong's and Poussin's editions and two commentaries, but not in the Tibetan translations or in several earlier commentaries; the other two verses are not in his edition. The DSBC text has the 12.6 verse in brackets (and the 23.20 verse), and has no 3.7. The aligned lists below show these verses as present in only one text.
